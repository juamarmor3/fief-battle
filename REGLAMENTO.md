# Reglamento — Fief Battle

Players execute this Phase in Turn Order


## 7.0 Initiating Battles
• Battles can take place in Cities where opposing Troops are
present.

•Opposing Players do not have to Battle each other and
can declare a Truce (7.8) which can be broken at any time.
Note: A Player may attack an Ally.

• Once a Battle has been declared, both sides reveal their Troops.

• Troops without a Noble cannot declare a Battle, but they will
defend themselves if attacked. Solo Nobles are captured(7.6).

• A declared Battle is resolved immediately. The Active Player
may then decide to declare another, and so on.

    Example 7-1: Dave and George are allied through Marriage (9.0) and occupy Coventry together. Kirsten moves her Troops to Coventry to attack Dave. She convinces George to join her side and he agrees. They combine their Troop Strength Points (7.3)and defeat Dave’s Army, slaying George’s spouse in the process.
Note: This is a subtle way to take care of a pesky husband. George is no longer allied to Dave

### Ally Attacks
If three or more Players have Troops in a City,
some may decide to fight together against other opposing Players. They combine their Troops and the Player with the most Strength Points (7.3)
decides which Troops take Hits and which Noble takes Captives. Allies may betray each other anytime during the Battle, even switching sides mid-Battle! If only opposing Nobles are in a City, they do not Battle.
The first to arrive in the City retains control of it.

## 7.1 Battle Progression
A Battle Round consists of:
 - Projectile Attacks
and
 - Melee Attacks
All Battle Dice are the same and have the following Faces:
- 1x Blank = 0 Hit
- 2x ‘f’ = 1 Hit
- 2x ‘ff’ = 2 Hits
- 1x ‘fff’ = 3 Hits
Only Battle Dice are used and rolled during Battles.
One Player rolls the White Battle Dice and their Opponent the Black Battle Dice.

## 7.2 Projectile Attacks
There are three types of Projectiles - Trebuchets, Bombards,and Archers/Crossbowmen.
Each Projectile type’s attacks are executed and losses are taken by both sides simultaneously,before the next type fires.
Trebuchets and Bombards can only be fired if at least one Men-at-Arms is in the City with them. Otherwise, they cannot be used.
If only Trebuchets, Bombards, and/or Nobles are in a City, they offer no defense. After the Battle, Trebuchets and Bombards can either be destroyed or absorbed into the Attacker’s side by replacing the Markers with their own. Nobles are captured.
Trebuchet and Bombard attacks are not affected by defender Stronghold or Fortified City Penalties (7.4).


### Trebuchets

Roll 2 Battle Die for a Trebuchet attack.
- Count the total number of Hits rolled. The Opponent takes losses (7.5)
- If two Trebuchets are attacking together, the second adds +1f Hit o the first Trebuchets’ 2 Battle Dice result.
Trebuchets can not move from the City they were built in.

### Bombards

Roll 1 Battle Die for each Bombard.

- Count the total number of Hits rolled. The Opponent takes losses (7.5)
- ‘Blank’ face roll results in an attacking Bombard exploding. For each ‘Blank’ face rolled, remove a Bombard and one attacking Men-at-Arms and place them back into their Stockpiles.

Bombards can move with an Army.

Tactical Destruction: A Player can destroy their own Trebuchets and Bombards at the end of a Battle Round, if they have Troops remaining.
Note: A Player may want to destroy their own Trebuchets and
Bombards so that:
 - They do not fall into enemy hands.
 - To have them available to build elsewhere since their numbers are limited.

Winner’s Spoils: The winner of a Battle can destroy or absorb their Opponent’s Trebuchets and Bombards by replacing the markers with their own.


### Archers & Crossbowmen

Roll 1 Battle Die for ALL attacking Archers/Crossbowmen.

- Count the total number of Hits rolled. The Opponent takes losses (7.5)
- Add +1f Bonus to the Die Result if firing with 3 or more Archers/Crossbowmen.
- Attacking a Stronghold requires at least 2 Archers/Crossbowmen and attacking a Fortified City requires at least 3 Archers/ Crossbowmen. They receive no +1f Bonus.
- Targeting a Noble: Archers/Crossbowmen can choose to target an opposing Noble or Invading Leader rather than Troops. They must be announced this before rolling.

## 7.3 Melee Attacks
Melee Attacks are executed and losses are taken by both sidessimultaneously.Each side counts up itsStrength Points(SPs).A Player receives:
- 1 SP per Men-at-Arms
- 1 SP per Archer
- 2 SP per Crossbowmen
- 3 SP per Knight
- 1 SP per male Noble
- 1 SP per female Titled Lord
 
 An Invader receives:
 - 2 SP per Foot Soldier
 - 3 SP per Leader 
 A side’s SP total determines the number of Battle Dice thatPlayer may roll.

## 7.4 Melee Attacker Penalties
 - -1 Battle Die if the defender is in a Stronghold
 - -2 Battle Dice if the defender is in a Fortified City
.
A PASSAGE Card (10.2) played by the attacker cancels all Attacker Penalties for that Battle! Attacker Penalties do not apply when the Army occupying a Stronghold or Fortified City attacks out at their besieging Opponent.

    Example 7-3: David occupies a Stronghold and is attacked by George who has a 15 SP Army. George’s 15 SPs = 3 Battle Dice. But he is attacking a Stronghold, which imposes a 1 Battle Die Penalty. George attacks with only 2 Battle Dice.

 ## 7.5 Taking Losses 
 Players roll their Battle Dice and losses are taken from both sides simultaneously. Each Player chooses which of their Units to remove, but must remove as many SPs as possible up to the number of Hits taken. 
 
 Note: Ally Attacks see 7.0 . Battle Dice results represent the number of Hits sustained in battle (7.1) .
 
 Each Troop Marker pictures Swords that represent the number of Hits required to defeat it. The number of Hits required to defeat each Troop are listed below: 
 - Men-at-Arms : 1 Hit 
 - Archers : 1 Hit 
 - Crossbowmen : 2 Hits 
 - Knights : 3 Hits 
 - Nobles : 1 Hit
 - Invader Foot Soldiers : 2 Hits
 - Invader Leaders : 3 Hits 
 
 Note: A Knight can only be defeated if 3 or more Hits are rolled. If only 1 or 2 Hits are rolled, a Knight is not affected. 
 All Hits that can be taken, must be taken. 
 Players can choose a mix of Men-at-Arms and Knights as casualties. Nobles are targeted only after all other Troops are defeated. So if there are insufficient Hits to defeat the last Knight standing, a Noble is not touched even if there are 1 or 2 Hits remaining. Defeated Troops are returned to the Player’s supply. Slain Nobles are removed from the game (4.7) .
 
     Example 7-3 Continued: George rolls 2 Battle Dice resulting in an f and fff, for a total of 4 Hits. David rolls at the same time. His 2 Men-at-Arms and 3 Knights have a total of 11 SPs = 2 Battle Dice. He rolls a fff and a ff = 5 Hits. David has no choice and must remove 1 Men-at-Arms and 1 Knight to match the 4 Hits. George removes 2 Men-at-Arms and 1 Knight. T roops remain on both sides. George wants to continue the Battle so a new Battle Round begins with Projectile Attacks. Capturing Nobles : Nobles are taken Captive when all of their Troops are defeated and there are no more unused Hits (7.6) . Ex 7-4: Kirsten is attacked and has the following defending Units: Lady Anabel, Lord Herbert, 1 Men-at-Arms, and 1 Knight. If her Opponent rolls: • 1 or 2 Hits - the first Hit defeats the Men-at-Arms. The second Hit is ignored, since it takes 3 Hits to defeat a Knight, and Nobles are not affected until all Troops with them are defeated. • 3 Hits - the Knight is defeated and the Men-at-Arms and Nobles are not affected. (If there were 1 Knight and 3 Men- at-Arms, the Player would choose which Troops to remove.) • 4 Hits - The Men-at-Arms and Knight are defeated, and both Nobles are taken Captive. • 5 Hits - All Troops are defeated, one Noble is slain, and the other Noble is taken Captive. • 6 Hits - All Troops are defeated and both Nobles are slain.
     
Reminder: Trebuchets and Bombards can only be destroyed at the end of a Battle, or when a Player destroys their own at the end of a Battle Roll, if they have Troops remaining (7.2) . 
Nobles, Trebuchets, and Bombards, alone in a City without Troops, are immediately captured by an Opponent Army


## 7.6 Captives 
When a Noble is taken captive, place their Standee on their Captor’s Noble Card or Invasion Captor’s Leader Marker. Captives still count towards a Player’s three Noble limit. The Captive is now under their Captor’s control and moves with them. 
Troops and Invader Foot Soldiers without a Noble or Leader do not take Captives; instead they immediately slay them (4.8) . 
A Captive keeps their Titles and Incomes, but cannot be named a candidate to become King or vote in any election or Parliament. 
A tax associated with their Title (2.8) may still be levied by a captive Noble (to collect money to pay their ransom, for example!).
Safe Captive : Once captured, a Noble may not be Slain by their captors, unless an ASSASSINATION Card (10.3) is played, even by another Player! If the Queen is captive, the Prince of Wales can still be put into play, but he is a captive with his mother. 
Escape : A Captive may escape using a PASSAGE Card (10.2) , even if played by another Player. 
Released Captives : A Captive can be set free by their Captor for any reason, including the payment of a Ransom (7.7) . 
A Captive is released if all of their Captor’s Troops are defeated in a Battle, if the Captor is Assassinated (10.3) , or if an Invader Captor leaves England (6.9, 6.10) . 
A released Captive’s Standee is placed in one of their Family’s Strongholds or Fortified Cities, or with one of their Family Nobles.

## 7.7 Ransom
Ransoms are paid at the beginning of the Purchase Phase (4.0) , before any other purchases, including new Troops, Buildings, or Titles.
A Captive’s Ransom is 2 Shillings, plus 2 Shillings per Title they hold. 
    Example 7-5: A Noble who is both a Bishop and a Baron is worth 6 Shillings. 
Paying a Ransom is mandatory, if the Player has enough money to pay the full price at the beginning of the Purchase Phase. A Captor must accept a full Ransom amount during the Purchase Phase and free the Captive. 
However, a Captor may accept or refuse a lower Ransom amount. (Negotiating for a lower Ransom payment does not require a Carrier Pigeon.) 
If the Player does not have enough Shillings to pay the full Ransom, he may then use all of his Shillings for other purchases. 

    Example 7-6: Dave ’s Lord Henri is George’s Captive . His Ransom value is 6 Shillings. At the beginning of the Purchase Phase, Dave has 5 Shillings. He may negotiate with George to pay less than 6 Shillings but if George disagrees, Dave cannot pay and Lord Henri remains a Captive. However, if Dave had 10 Shillings and he could not negotiate with George to play less than 6 Shillings, then Dave would have had to pay the full amount and George would have to accept the money and free Lord Henri.

A freed Noble’s Standee is placed in any of their Family’s Strongholds or Fortified Cities, or with any of their Family Nobles

## 7.8 End of Battle 
A Battle can last several Battle Rounds of Projectile and Melee attacks. A Battle ends in one of the following ways: 
- Victory : One side’s Army is defeated and its Nobles were either slain or captured. OR 
- Surrender : At the end of a Battle Dice Roll, one Army can surrender. All remaining Troops of the surrendering Army are taken off the Map and its Nobles are taken Captive. OR 
- Withdraw: With a PASSAGE Card (10.2) , a Defender may move their Nobles and allied Nobles out of a Battle to an adjacent City. Each Noble may take one of their Troops with them. All remaining Troops must remain in the Battle. OR 
- Truce : At the end of a Battle Dice Roll, both sides can declare a Truce. All Nobles and Troops from both Armies remain in the City and the original Player that controlled the City keeps control. However, control of the City can be transfered between Players as part of the Truce agreement.
- Assassination : One side has no Noble, leaving their Troops without a Leader. At the end of a Battle Dice Roll, the opposing Army can alone decide to end the Battle. 
- Annihilation : If only Nobles remain on both sides, they all remain in the City and the Battle is over. City control does not change. 
- Deadlock : 3 Battle Dice rolls in a row result in no losses. Both Armies remain in the City and City control does not change. 

Note: A Player attacking an Opponent in the safety of a Stronghold or a Fortified City can alone decide to end the Battle. Both sides remain in the City. The defending Opponent can continue the Battle on their Turn by giving up the safety of their Stronghold or Fortified City and fighting in the ope

## 7.9 Siege
Begin a Siege : A Player attacking a defended Stronghold or Fortified City can stop their attack and declare a Siege, after any Battle Roll or if they do not have enough SPs to roll at least 1 Battle Die. The Besieger places a Trebuchet, pulled from their Stockpile, on their attacking Army - ‘ Under Construction ’ side face-up. If a Player has no Trebuchet available in their Stockpile to place, they may destroy one of their Trebuchets on the map to place on the new Siege site. 
Construct Trebuchets : In the next Round during Phase 4 - Purchasing, the Besieger must pay 5 Shilling to construct the Trebuchet, turning it ‘ Active ’ side up. The Besieger can place a second ‘Active’ Trebuchet by paying an additional 5 Shillings. If the Besieger does not pay for any Trebuchet, it is removed from the Map and the Siege is lifted . 

Besieged : 
- The City remains under the control of the Besieged Player. 
- City and Mill Revenues are not collected .
- Purchased Troops cannot be placed in a besieged City. 
- New Nobles may be placed in a besieged City (2.11) . 
- Troops and Nobles can not leave the City without the Besieger’s permission, unless a PASSAGE Card is played (10.2) . or if they make a Cavalcade move (5.2) . 
- May move additional Troops directly into the besieged Stronghold or Fortified City from an adjacent City without having to fight through the Besiegers

Besieger:
- May move additional Troops to join their Besieging Army.
- May end the Siege at any time by removing all Trebuchets.
- If no besieging Noble is present, the Siege is automatically lifted and all Trebuchets are returned to the Stockpile.

Sally: The Besieged may, on their Turn, initiate a Melee Battle,
but in doing so they lose the protection of the Stronghold or
Fortified City. During the Sally, neither side may use Bombards
or Trebuchets. The Sally is conducted like normal battles, except
that the Projectile Phase is skipped. After a Battle Roll, the
Besieged may stop their Sally Attack and return any surviving
Troops to the safety of their Stronghold or Fortified City

## 7.10 Pillage
When all Battles are resolved, Players (in Player order) may pillage Cities they control. 
Mills may be destroyed (removed from the Map). Collect 2 Shillings per Mill. 
Abbeys can be destroyed. Collect 10 Shillings per Abbey destroyed, take any Relics located in the Abbey (12.0) , and mark the pillaging Noble with a Heresy Marker. 
Trebuchets and Bombards of either side may be destroyed. 
Opponent Trebuchets and Bombards may be exchanged with those of the Pillager, if available. Otherwise they have to be destroyed and returned to the Stockpil
